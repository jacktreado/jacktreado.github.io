The spongy mesophyll, a complex porous tissue found within leaves and flowers,
captures atmospheric carbon during photosynthesis and provides important
structural stability. But how does this complex tissue develop its vital airspace?

Using deformable particles, we show that airspace can emerge from growth and
remodelling of cell walls near void space.

The colorbar in the simulation represents the shape parameter
\\( {\\cal A} = p^2 / 4\\pi a \\), where \\(p\\) is the cell perimeter and
\\(a\\) is the cell area.
