Complex soft solids like emulsions (e.g. mayonnaise), foams (e.g. cappuccino
froth) and tissues (e.g. your epidermis) are composed of inherently
deformable objects like droplets, bubbles or cells, yet how deformability
affects collective behavior is poorly understood. In our recent paper, we
studied the effect of single-particle deformability on global vibrational
and mechanical response near jamming. We found that stiff, weakly
deformable particles reach a rigid-particle limit, but increasing
deformability leads to anomalous normal mode spectra and shear moduli.

Shown here are examples of normal modes found in jammed packings of highly
deformable particles. These packings contain a few "quartic modes" which
are higher-order and much floppier than standard, quadratic modes. Floppy
quartic modes can be either extended (black, left) or localized (black,
right), while the quadratic modes (red and blue) vary in extent, but
predominantly change particle shape.
